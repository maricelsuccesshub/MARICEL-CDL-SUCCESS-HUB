## Verified distinctions and references

| Teaching point | Primary reference | Interpretation retained in Hub |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting an intentional trailer turn versus correcting an unwanted straight-back drift | [Oregon CDL Manual §6.1.7](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/DMV/Pages/Online_CDL_Manual_2/Section_6.aspx) | From an aligned combination, initial steering for a desired trailer-rear turn is opposite that intended direction; steering then changes to follow. Initial small correction for confirmed straight-back drift is toward the drift. These are different starting situations, not universal instructions for an already-angled setup. |
| Preparation, mirrors, slow control, path inspection and preferred visibility | [Oregon CDL Manual §2.2.4](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/DMV/Pages/Online_CDL_Manual_2/Section_2.aspx) and §6.1.7 | Inspect the path and clearances, check both mirrors, prefer driver-side backing where possible, use equipment-specific controls, stop when unsure and secure before leaving the cab. |
| Spotter communication and lost visual contact | [OSHA preventing backovers — spotters](https://www.osha.gov/preventing-backovers/solutions) | Agree on signals beforehand; use a trained visible helper outside movement paths and pinch points; stop immediately on loss of visual contact. This page provides guidance; it does not establish a universal OSHA mandate for every backing situation. |
| Fifth wheel and kingpin | [Oregon CDL Manual §6.4](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/DMV/Pages/Online_CDL_Manual_2/Section_6.aspx) | The fifth-wheel coupling and locking mechanism secure the kingpin. A plate alone is not described as the lock. Coupling inspection is outside these backing lessons. |
| Current test layouts and finish instructions | [Oregon CDL Manual §12](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/dmv/pages/online_cdl_manual_2/section_12.aspx); [California current commercial-driver information](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/driver-education-and-safety/special-interest-driver-guides/truck-drivers/commercial-driver-information/) | Current testing can differ from traditional adjacent-lane offset/alley examples. No nationwide cone triggers, wheel-turn counts, permitted looks/pull-ups, finish line or test scoring is prescribed. Confirm current state and testing-location instructions. California describes modernized testing at select locations. |
| General California backing guidance | [California DMV Truck Drivers](https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/driver-education-and-safety/special-interest-driver-guides/truck-drivers/) | Used for general backing principles, not as sole authority for the current test layout. |
| Actual loading dock versus marked training bay | [OSHA dockboards §1910.26](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.26) | Hub refers to approved site/equipment procedures, door handling, confirmed dock readiness and restraints. It does not teach a universal docking/restraint procedure or permission to move against restraints. |

## Corrections made

- Replaced public teaching/download of the supplied poster. Its unconditional promises of mastery in every situation and centered-wheel straightness were too broad. The corrected, self-contained SVG study sheet carries directional context, stop/secure/pull-up distinctions and limitations within the downloaded file. An accessible HTML text companion remains at `/backing-poster` with full-size, download and print options.
- Preserved the original supplied JPEG at `source-materials/original-backing-visual-system.jpeg`, outside the deployed static directory. This archive is not presented as current teaching material.
- Removed Practice Yard's displayed numerical trailer angles, move counter/limit and implied fixed steering increments. Tests 1/2 now assess the initial drift-correction decision using preset pictures with explicit limits: no physical movement, angle, speed, timing or clearance is calculated, and one click is not a wheel turn. Wrong/hold choices explain the distinction and allow retry without inventing a simulated physical result. The later aligned picture is explicitly a separate teaching example, not proof that one real input straightens the truck.
- Corrected “behind the trailer's heading” to alignment **with** that heading; clarified fifth-wheel terminology and low-speed wording.
- Clarified that pre-trip snippets are reminders, not complete inspections or brake-test procedures; GOAL starts with stopping and securing; a two-hour sunset buffer is a planning aid rather than a safety rule, legal deadline or parking guarantee. The destination tool already labels its buffer appropriately.
- Added current test information alongside general backing references. Updated teaching reference review dates. Preserved all existing Maricel tractor/trailer artwork and the distinction between driver direction, screen direction and generic mirror examples.

## Geometry and beginner checks

- Top-view artwork has the tractor front facing down: seated-driver right is screen left, and driver left is screen right. Right/left drift illustrations and mirrored offset-side directions were checked against that orientation.
- Rear corners, rear wheel group, tractor front, opposite-side clearance, lane entrance, hidden rear path and intended stopping area remain separate references. No single corner or mirror picture confirms the whole vehicle's clearance.
- Tractor-to-trailer alignment differs from trailer-to-lane alignment; parallel can still be off-center. Wheel center alone proves neither alignment.
- The 45° and 90° guide titles describe approaches relative to the bay, not prescribed articulation or wheel angles. Blind-side guidance emphasizes limited visibility and alternatives rather than a guaranteed steering method.
- Guide poses are separate illustrative examples, not an engineered path or turning-radius calculation. They do not establish a real safe maximum articulation, cone layout, scale or stopping distance.
- Questions give explanations and retries; review/completion marks are screen learning only, not practical driving competence, certification or proof of readiness. Tests 4 and 5 remain unimplemented.

## Practical limits / instructor responsibility

This review supports the consistency and sourcing of the educational content; it cannot establish that every lesson applies unchanged to every vehicle, load, site or student. Coach Maricel or another qualified instructor must demonstrate actual controls, visible references, response, timing, setup, safe stopping and recovery on the equipment used. Physical yard execution, exact current local exam instructions and later changes in official sources require practical/current verification. Do not represent this review as a safety guarantee, official approval or assurance that the material can never be questioned.
