Pre-trip planning reference

Low Bridges & Clearance Checks

Find official low-bridge, vertical-clearance and truck-restriction resources before your trip.

Verify during pre-trip—and along the route. Confirm the actual total height of your truck, trailer and load, including the highest equipment or accessory, using your company’s approved procedures and qualified help when needed. Do not assume every trailer is the same height. A completed pre-trip does not prove a bridge is safe to pass.

Coach Maricel’s clearance check

1 · Know your actual height

Confirm the complete vehicle/load height during pre-trip and whenever equipment, load or operating configuration changes. Never climb onto a vehicle or load to measure without appropriate equipment and procedures.

2 · Verify your full route

Check official clearance records and current restrictions for the correct road, direction and lane. Include ramps, detours, local streets and the final approach. Obtain required permits and carrier guidance.

3 · Read the signs

Roadwork, resurfacing, snow and changing conditions can affect available space. Obey posted restrictions. If clearance is uncertain, stop safely before the obstruction and seek qualified assistance; do not attempt to squeeze through.

Use with caution, at your own discretion and risk. This Hub is not a complete bridge inventory, truck GPS, route survey, clearance certification or substitute for pre-trip inspection, professional instruction, company procedures, posted signs or official requirements. No listing—or absence of a listing—guarantees safe passage. Pre-trip verification is necessary but does not replace checking each structure and current restrictions. The driver/operator must independently verify suitability.

Coach Maricel’s winter trip check

1 · Check the time

Read the camera timestamp and viewing direction. An old or frozen image is not a current road report.

2 · Look along the route

Compare several nearby views for visibility and changing conditions. A clear-looking image cannot rule out ice.

3 · Check restrictions

Read official closures, wind alerts and commercial-vehicle or chain restrictions before deciding whether to proceed.

Truck Parking & Rest Stops

Choose your state and open its travel map directly, or use an established truck-stop locator below.

Check rest-area status on the map, then confirm truck access, fees and overnight rules for your stop. Available parking spaces are not guaranteed.

Your city is shown as a reminder below. Enter it in the operator’s locator after opening its website.

Before choosing an overnight stop:
  • Confirm wide truck entrance.
  • Check satellite view.
  • Avoid tiny gas stations, low bridges, dead ends, and tight shopping centers.
  • Confirm the location accepts your truck and permits your intended rest period.
  • A shipper, receiver, distribution center, fuel pump, or scale is not overnight parking unless the operator explicitly permits it.
  • Never risk unsafe shoulder parking.

Safety & Resources

Quick reminders to review before practice or a trip. Follow your instructor and official guidance.

Pre-Trip Reminder
Review tires, lights, brakes, leaks, coupling, mirrors, fluids, paperwork and safety equipment. This is a reminder only, not a complete inspection or brake-test procedure. Follow your instructor, equipment guidance and current inspection checklist.
GOAL Rule
When unsure, stop and secure the vehicle before getting out to look. Inspect the full intended path, including the sides and overhead space.
Parking Before Dark
Plan parking early, before fatigue, duty limits, weather or darkness restrict your options. A sunset buffer is a planning aid, not a safety cutoff or a parking-availability guarantee.
Need Help Now
Stop in a suitable location and secure using the equipment’s parking procedure. Breathe. Check surroundings. Contact your instructor or dispatch for guidance; use emergency services for an emergency.

Critical test area: every required check must be correct.

The California and Washington modernized guides require all four air-brake checks to be explained and performed correctly; failure to do so causes automatic failure of the Vehicle Inspection test. Know the applicable limits, demonstrate the correct procedure and report the actual observations. Confirm your state/test-site format and vehicle specifications before practice.
